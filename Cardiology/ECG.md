- Normal range = -30 degrees (aVL) and 90 degrees (aVF)
- QRS terminology: all up waves = R waves, all down waves *below isoelectric line* = S waves EXCEPT first one which is q wave

Approach:
- Rate (tachy or brady? 300/big boxes)
	- Think 300, 150, 100, 75, 60, 50
- Rhythm (P wave -> QRS?, regular?)
- Intervals (PR, QRS, QT)
	- Remember PR + QT interval = one cycle
	- QT interval = QRS + ST interval
	- PR and ST segments are the flat isoelectric sections between waves
- Axis
	- Look leads I and aVF where up thumb = axis

Left Axis Deviation:
- Etiologies: LVH, LBBB, inferior MI, WPW

Right Axis Deviation:
- Etiologies: RVH, PE, COPD, lateral MI, WPW

BBB:
- Both require prolonged QRS >120 msec (technically 110-119 msec can be incomplete)
- Right:
	- rSR' in right precordial leads (V1, V2)
	- Wide S wave in I and V6
	- Sometimes ST depression or TWI in right precordial leads (V1, V2)
- Left:
	- Broad, slurred, monophasic R (or notched Rr') in I, aVL, V5, V6
	- No q wave in lateral leads (I, V5, V6)

Prolonged QT Interval:
- Start of QRS to end of T wave < 450 msec male, 460 msec female
- Etiologies:
	- Antiarrhythmics: Class Ia (procainamide, disopyramide), Class III (amio, sotalol, dofet/tikosyn)
	- Psych drugs: Antipsychotics (phenothiazines, haloperidol, atypicals), lithium, TCA
	- Antimicrobials: Macrolides, quinolones, azoles, pentamidine, atazanavir
	- Other: Antiemetics (droperidol, 5-HT3 like ondansetron/zofran), alfuzosin, methadone, ranolazine
	- Electrolytes: HypoCa, hyperCa with decreased QT, hypoK/hypoMag sometimes
	- Autonomic dysfunction: ICH (deep TWI), Takotsubo, stroke, CEA, neck dissection
	- Congenital: Long QT syndrome, K/Na/Ca channelopathies
	- Misc: CAD, CMP, bradycardia, high-grade AVB, hypothyroidism, hypothermia, BBB

LVH:
- Cornell: R in aVL + S in V3 
	- >28 mm in men and >20 in women
